What pipeline reveals

Most pipeline issues are readiness issues in disguise.

  • 01
    Pipeline leans on existing customers.

    Most opportunities come from accounts already won. Comfortable, but it caps growth at the rate the existing base expands. Net-new acquisition is underbuilt, not because outbound is bad, but because the offering hasn't been packaged for someone who doesn't already trust you.

  • 02
    The opportunity exists, but isn't shaped for AWS.

    A real deal sits in the CRM with no business challenge, technical scope, or timeline that AWS can engage around. Co-sell stays one-sided, and the partner closes alone.

  • 03
    The problem is real, but the offering isn't shaped to it.

    The customer has a challenge worth solving. Without a defined first deliverable, a quantified outcome, and a clear path forward, the conversation drifts. A packaging gap, not a sales gap.

  • 04
    The value isn't articulated for the buyer.

    The offering gets described in infrastructure terms, services, instances, regions, when the buyer needs to hear it in business terms: cost per customer, time to market, availability. The translation hasn't happened yet.

  • 05
    Outreach reaches the wrong level, or the right level at the wrong time.

    The conversation is friendly, but the person on the other end can't sponsor a project right now. Either the audience hasn't been defined sharply, or the trigger that makes the right persona engage hasn't been identified.

The AWS reality

Co-sell is not a tool.
It's a human motion.

Co-sell was designed as structured collaboration. For most partners, it behaves like late-stage validation. AWS engages when an opportunity is already commercially clear, technically credible, and easy to justify internally. That's the bar the programme was built around. Most opportunities never reach it.

  • PDM Your strategic counterpart inside AWS. Champions partners whose work moves the partnership forward.
  • AM Owns the customer relationship at AWS. Engages partners whose deals build real momentum in the account.
  • SA Brings the technical depth that makes the customer confident. Engages where there's a solution worth designing for.
  • ACE The registration that surfaces the AWS account team and kicks off the working relationship.

Co-sell happens when the opportunity is shaped for AWS internally and delivers a real outcome for the customer.

Outbound creates access. Shape creates engagement.

This is how the AWS partner ecosystem was designed to work. Partners shape opportunities AWS can stand behind. Account teams hit their numbers. Customers get the outcomes the programmes were built for.

The commitment

Five qualified opportunities a month.

What qualified means Commercially: a named decision-maker with budget authority, a confirmed customer outcome the engagement will deliver, and an agreed first meeting. For AWS: a problem scope tied to a specific AWS service or motion, with enough structure for the opportunity to enter ACE cleanly, support the AM's quarter, and give your PDM something to build on.

If after three months the work hasn't reached that standard, the engagement continues until it has.
